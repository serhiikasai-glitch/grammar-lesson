Possessive ’s

To show who something belongs to, English uses possessive ’s, answering the question whose?. For example, the possessive is needed in expressions such as “This is John’s ball” or “This is Kate’s pen”, which say who something belongs to.

So, in the phrases “John’s ball” and “Kate’s pen”, the names John and Kate take the possessive form because those people own the things. The possessive noun goes before the thing that belongs to its owner.

To make the singular possessive, add an apostrophe (’) and s: Michael => Michael’s, boy => boy’s.

For plural nouns ending in -s , make the possessive by adding only an apostrophe (’): my brothers => my brothers’.

Nouns that do not end in -s in the plural take the possessive ending s: children => children’s

So the possessive forms for people and animals covered in this lesson have these endings:

SingularPlurals
John’s ball — John’s ball

Kate’s pen — Kate’s pen 		my brothers’ bike — my brothers’ bicycle

my children’s room — my children’s room

To ask who something belongs to, use the question word whose whose.

A relationship between things can often be expressed using the preposition of: the door of the room – door room.

Here are examples from this lesson:

This is John’s ball.
This is the ball of John.
This is my sisters’ doll.
This is the doll of my sisters.
Whose purse is this?
Whose purse is this?
The window of this room is big.
Window of this room is big.
