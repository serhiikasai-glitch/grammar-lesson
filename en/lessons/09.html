Questions with the verb to be

Question forms

Questions with the verb to be are made as follows: put the verb to be before the subject. This gives us the following forms:

Am I ? Are we ?
Are you ?Are you ?
     he
Is  she ?
     it 		Are they ?

Here are examples of the verb to be in questions:

Am I from Moscow? — Am I from Moscow?
Is he from London? — Is he from London?
Are you from the USA? - You of the USA?

Answering yes/no questions with to be

Yes/no questions are questions that can be answered with “yes” or “no”. Here are some examples: Is this a book? — Yes. Is he from Canada? — No.

You can answer with yes or no in English, but a short answer usually adds a pronoun and the correct positive or negative form of the verb.

Positive answers look like this:

Are you ...? - Yes, I am. Are you ...? - Yes, we are.
Am I ...? - Yes, you are. Are we ...? - Yes, you are.
     he
Is  she...? - Yes, she/he/it is.
     it 		Are they ...? - Yes, they are.

Negative answers look like this:

Are you ...? - No, I'm not. Are you ...? - No, we aren't.
Am I ...? - No, you aren't. Are we ...? - No, you aren't.
     he
Is  she...? - No, she/he/it isn't.
     it 		Are they ...? - No, they aren't.

Here are examples of answers to yes/no questions:

Are you from Russia? - Yes, I am.
Are you from Russia? — Yes.
Is he Russian? - No, he isn't
Is he Russian? - No.
