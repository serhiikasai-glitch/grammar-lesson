The verb to be

Using the verb to be

English often uses the verb to be to be. It means “to exist” or describes what someone or something is. Some languages often leave out this verb in the present tense, but English normally needs it.

For example, in some languages the phrase “I am a pupil” can have no verb. The equivalent English sentence must include a verb:I is a pupil” or “I am a pupil".

For now, remember: a normal English statement needs a verb. If the equivalent sentence in another language has no verb, English will often use to be to be.

Conjugating to be

The verb to be changes its form to match the person and number of the subject. This is called conjugation, as with the verb to be: I am, you are, he is and so on. Here are the forms of to be in the present tense.

I amwe are
you areyou are
he
she  is
it 		they are

So to be has three present-tense forms: am, is and are.

I am a boy. - I boy.
She is a student. — She is a student.
We are doctors. - We are doctors.

In conversation, the forms of to be can be contracted: am => 'm, is => 's, are => 're.

I'm a boy. - I boy.
She's a student. — She is a student.
We're doctors. - We are doctors.
