Pattern have got

Meaning and use

To express possession, English uses the pattern have got. Its meaning can be expressed as I have (you have, he has, and so on) or, in the negative, I do not have (you do not have, he does not have, and so on).

Notice that after the pattern have got singular countable nouns normally take a/an; plural nouns do not.

I have got an apple.
I have got an apple.
They have got paints.
They have got paints.

Positive forms

Pattern have got has these forms:

SingularPlurals
I have gotwe have got
you have gotyou have got
he/she/it has gotthey have got

Contractions are common in conversation:

SingularPlurals
I've gotwe've got
you've gotyou've got
he/she/it's gotthey've got

Negative forms

To make a negative sentence with have got add not:

SingularPlurals
I have not gotwe have not got
you have not gotyou have not got
he/she/it has not gotthey have not got

The contracted negative forms are:

SingularPlurals
I haven't gotwe haven't got
you haven't gotyou haven't got
he/she/it hasn't gotthey haven't got
