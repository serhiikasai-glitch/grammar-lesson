Adverbs of frequency

Adverbs of frequency are words that show how often an action happens, or whether it happens at all. Examples include:

always - always
usually - usually
often - often
seldom - seldom
sometimes — sometimes
never - never

Remember that adverbs of frequency normally go before the main verb.

I always wash my car. – I always wash the car.

She usually walks to school. – She usually walks to school.

We often play sports. – We often play sports.

He seldom eats chocolate. – He seldom eats chocolate.

I sometimes play computer games. – I sometimes play computer games.

They never dance. – They never dance.

Notice that in an English sentence containing never the verb has a positive form: do not add another negative word.

I never drink coffee. – I never drink coffee.

In negative sentences and questions, adverbs of frequency also go before the main verb.

I don't often eat pizza. – I do not often eat pizza.

Do you sometimes watch cartoons? – Do you sometimes watch cartoons?

In English, instead of seldom to mean “rarely”, speakers often use often in a negative form.

I don't often sing songs. - I do not often sing songs.
