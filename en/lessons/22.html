The simple present tense (Present Simple)

Verbs can describe past, present or future actions, for example: played (past), I play (present) or I will play (future). We will start with verbs in the present tense.

In this lesson, learn to describe repeated actions and lasting situations. Examples of repeated actions: I go to school, I often read books; lasting situations: I like sport, I work as a doctor.

To describe repeated actions and lasting situations, English uses the tense called present Simple or Present Simple. Let us look at this tense using the verb to like to like.

To use a verb in Present Simple , normally use the base form. Only the third-person singular adds -s. In some cases, the third-person singular adds -es, which we will discuss in the next lesson.

The verb to like

The verb to like means finding someone or something pleasant. For the stronger feeling of loving a person, use to love. For things, food and activities, like expresses enjoyment or a favourable opinion.

I like pizza.
I like pizza.
He doesn't like this T-shirt.
He does not like this shirt.
I like this girl.
I like this girl.

If the verb to like is followed by another verb, the next verb can be an infinitive with to.

She likes to swim.
She likes to swim.
You don't like to read.
You do not like to read.
