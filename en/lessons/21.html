Verbs in English

English has words for actions, called verbs. A verb’s base form is the infinitive, often shown after the word to. This is the form normally used to introduce verbs in a vocabulary list, for example:

to swim — to swim
to read – to read
to dance – to dance

Notice that here the word to is an infinitive marker, rather than a separate part of the meaning.

The modal verb can

Meaning and use

Some English verbs belong to a special group called modal verbs. They express ideas such as ability or possibility rather than an action itself. One of these verbs is can, which can mean to know how to or to be able to. It describes the ability or possibility of doing an action.

The verb can is followed by the base form of another verb without to.

I can write.
I can to write.
She can sing.
She can to sing.
She cannot drive a car.
She cannot drive a car.

Forms

The verb can has the same form for every subject:

SingularPlurals
I canwe can
you canyou can
he/she/it canthey can

To make a negative, add to the verb can the word not:

SingularPlurals
I cannotwe cannot
you cannotyou cannot
he/she/it cannotthey cannot

Contracted negatives are common in conversation:

SingularPlurals
I can'twe can't
you can'tyou can't
he/she/it can'tthey can't

In questions, the verb can goes before the subject:

SingularPlurals
Can I ?Can we?
Can you?Can you?
Can he/she/it?Can they?

Short answers to questions with can look like this:

QuestionYESNO
Can you ...?Yes, I/we can. No, I/we can't.
Can he/she/it/they ...?Yes, he/she/it/they can. No, he/she/it/they can't.
Can I/we ...?Yes, you can. No, you can't.
